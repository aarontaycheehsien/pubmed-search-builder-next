## Rationale

The strategy searches nursing students and virtual/immersive educational technology as two AND-ed blocks. Comparator, learning outcomes, and controlled-study design remain screening criteria because requiring any of them could exclude eligible studies that do not name them consistently in PubMed fields. The nursing block combines the exploded `Students, Nursing` and `Education, Nursing` headings with student, nurse-student, nursing-education, prelicensure, pre-registration, and postgraduate wording. The intervention block combines the exploded `Virtual Reality`, `Simulation Training`, and `Computer Simulation` headings with virtual reality, virtual simulation, immersive simulation/technology, virtual patients/environments/worlds, computer/web-based simulation, 3D, virtual simulator, head-mounted display, and the common `VR` acronym. No `:noexp` headings, outcome/design filters, language limits, or animal exclusions were used. The broad simulation headings and acronym may add noise; they were retained to protect sensitivity and require screening.

The MeSH ablations showed that `Simulation Training` added 1,741 records (2,600 to 859) and `Computer Simulation` added 269 (2,331 to 2,600); neither uniquely retrieved any of the 17 development records. The headings remain because eligible digital or immersive instruction may be indexed under generic simulation headings or described without the listed VR wording. This is a deliberate sensitivity choice with a substantial screening burden, especially for `Simulation Training`.

The only limit is publication date through 2023-03-31, required by the task's evidence cutoff. The generated PubMed evaluation reported 2,600 records after this limit and 2,605 before it. The final counts were freshly rerun through `psb report --fresh`.

## How known records were found

No user-supplied seeds were available. A topic-specific PubMed systematic-review query returned 14 records; six review records were fetched and examined. References linked from two topic reviews yielded five primary-study candidates; their abstracts were screened, and two met the review criteria. A precise PubMed pilot for nursing students, VR terms, and controlled-trial terms returned 52 records; up to 40 were sampled, and 19 title-selected candidates had abstracts fetched and screened. Fifteen of those 19 met the criteria; alongside the two review-reference records, this produced 17 unique development records. Candidates that were reviews, uncontrolled, focused on nursing assistants rather than nursing students, or had mixed professional groups without separately clear nursing-student results were excluded or left uncertain. All 17 records were used for development; no held-out validation or benchmark set was available, and no record was held out. The reported 100% relative recall is development recall only, not independent validation or sensitivity.

## Open risks for the peer reviewer

- Terms such as virtual simulation, immersive simulation, and virtual patient are used inconsistently and may include screen-based or non-immersive interventions; eligibility needs full-text screening.
- `Simulation Training` and especially `Computer Simulation` are broad headings, and `VR[tiab]` is an ambiguous acronym. The headings add many records without unique development-set coverage; they were retained for recall.
- The 17 development records were discovered through PubMed topic reviews and a PubMed pilot, so the set is not independent and may reflect the search's vocabulary.
- Mixed-profession studies may contain eligible nursing learners but lack separately reported nursing-student outcomes; the protocol's extractability rule needs consistent application.
- This PubMed-only draft still needs PRESS peer review by an information specialist and should be translated/adapted for the other databases in the review.
